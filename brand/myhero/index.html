KICKSTART MEETING · 15 AUG 2026

Four decisions
that govern this book

Everything on the pages that follow traces back to one of these four. Where a later decision conflicts with one of them, this page wins.

DECISION 01

Keep the original colour scheme

Sky blue stays primary, blush pink stays secondary. The proposed all-new sky-blue palette was declined in favour of continuity.

DECISION 02

The doctor reads professional

The hero icon must look credible and trustworthy. No cartoon, no childish superhero, no gaming aesthetic anywhere in the system.

NO CARTOON
DECISION 03

A records app, not an AI app

Medical record management is the product. AI is a supporting technology that removes manual work — it never leads the story.

RECORD FIRST
DECISION 04

Sell the app, not the founder

The brand leads with what the app uniquely does for a family. Personal branding supports it; it does not replace it.

USP LEADS
ALSO AGREED

App name lands on My Health Record as the descriptive full name — MyHero is the brand it is spoken and searched as. Target audience narrows to young parents and mothers tracking a pregnancy at the clinic. The interface must feel warm and friendly without ever resembling a government portal.

POSITIONING
THE POSITIONING STATEMENT

For young families in care at Elite Care Clinic, MyHero is the health record that keeps every visit, result and milestone in one calm, trusted place — so parents walk into every appointment already knowing where they stand.

THE PROBLEM TODAY

Care is scattered. A booklet in one drawer, a lab slip in a bag, a date half-remembered from a phone call. Parents arrive at appointments unsure what was said last time, and re-tell the same history to every new clinician.

WHAT MYHERO CHANGES

One record per family member, filled as care happens rather than typed up afterwards. Results explained in plain words. The next date always visible. Nothing to file, nothing to lose.

WHY IT'S BELIEVABLE

The record comes from the clinic that made it. MyHero isn't a third party guessing at your health — it's Elite Care Clinic handing you your own file, in a form you can actually read.

BRAND IDEA & PILLARS
THE BRAND IDEA

The hero is
the parent.

The name doesn't promise a rescuer. It hands the role over. MyHero is the thing a parent carries so they can be the one who knows, who remembers, who asks the right question at the right moment.

This is why the brand never talks down, never dramatises, and never makes the technology the protagonist. It equips. It doesn't perform.

Record first

The record is the product. Complete, accurate, always to hand. Every feature must make the record better or get out of the way.

Plain language

Medical truth in human words. If a sentence would need a clinician to decode it, it is not finished.

Warm authority

Clinical credibility with a friendly face. Never cold, never childish. The tone of a good doctor who has time for you.

Quiet intelligence

AI works in the background to remove typing and filing. It never takes the stage, and it never speaks in place of a clinician.

AUDIENCE
WHO WE ARE DESIGNING FOR

Young parents,
mid-appointment

The audience was deliberately narrowed at kickstart. Designing for everyone produced a government portal; designing for this person produces MyHero.

PRIMARY

Parents of young children, 28–40

Managing immunisations, growth checks and the small emergencies of early childhood. They are competent and busy, and their memory is the weakest link in the chain of care.

WHAT THEY SAY

"I know she had it done — I just can't remember when, or what the doctor said afterwards."

SECONDARY

Expectant mothers tracking a pregnancy

Following scans, results and weekly milestones at the clinic. High emotional stakes, high appetite for detail, low tolerance for anything that feels careless or alarming.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCE

Blush is reserved almost entirely for this journey — milestones, memories and moments of care. It never signals an error.

TERTIARY

Clinic staff at the desk

Nurses and front-desk staff who add to the record between patients. They need speed and density where parents need calm and space.

DESIGN CONSEQUENCE

Staff surfaces may use tighter type and the mono numerals. Parent surfaces never do.

THIS SHOULD FEEL LIKE

  • A clinic that remembers you
  • A file you're glad to have on your phone
  • Someone competent, on your side

NEVER LIKE

  • A government portal or an insurance form
  • A children's game or a superhero cartoon
  • A machine making promises about your health

PERSONALITY
WHERE THE BRAND SITS

Professional and
cheerful — in that order

Cheerful is the finish, not the foundation. Credibility is established first; warmth is what makes it approachable. Reverse the order and the brand stops being believable.

CLINICALWARM
SERIOUSCHEERFUL
INSTITUTIONALPERSONAL
QUIETBOLD
TRADITIONALMODERN
IF MYHERO WERE A PERSON

The doctor who sits down before speaking. Precise about the facts, unhurried in the telling, and genuinely pleased to see your child again. Confident enough not to need jargon.

THE CHEERFUL 20%

Warmth arrives through rounded geometry, generous white space, blush accents on milestones and copy that speaks to a person — not through illustration style, exclamation marks or bright novelty colour.

THE TEST

Would a paediatrician be comfortable with this on the wall of their consultation room? If not, it is too playful. Would a new mother feel spoken to rather than processed? If not, it is too cold.

VOICE & TONE
HOW MYHERO WRITES

Say it the way
you'd say it out loud

Four rules cover almost every string in the product: lead with the person, keep to one idea per sentence, name the date plainly, and never dramatise a result.

SITUATIONWRITE THISNOT THIS
Upcoming appointment"Your next scan is on Wednesday 3 September, 10:30am.""Appointment scheduled: 03/09/2026 — Ultrasound (OB), Rm 4."
A normal result"Everything in this test looked normal.""No abnormalities detected in the submitted parameters."
A result needing follow-up"Dr Amira would like to go through one of these with you at your next visit.""⚠ ABNORMAL VALUE FLAGGED — action required immediately."
An automated reminder"We'll remind you two days before.""Automated notification protocol enabled for this event."
AI-assisted summary"Filled in from your visit notes — checked by your clinic.""AI-generated clinical summary powered by machine learning."
Empty state"Nothing here yet. Your clinic will add to this after your next visit.""No records found. 0 results returned."

ALWAYS

  • Second person — "your daughter", not "the patient"
  • Weekday and month spelled out for parents
  • State who did something: the clinic, the doctor, you

NEVER

  • Exclamation marks, emoji or hero language in clinical copy
  • Alarming a parent that a clinician has not yet spoken to
  • Superlatives about the technology — "smart", "powerful", "revolutionary"

CONCEPT ROUTES — TEMPORARY
● IN PROGRESSFOR DISCUSSION ONLY

Three directions,
one brief

Each route answers the same test: credible to a clinician, warm to a parent, legible at 24px, and impossible to mistake for a game.

ROUTE A

Guardian

A shield holding a medical cross. Protection and clinical care in one shape, with rounded geometry to keep it from reading as a security badge.

Strength — most immediately credible; scales to 16px without loss.

Risk — shields are common in health tech; needs distinctive proportions to stand apart.

ROUTE B

The Kept Record

A record card holding a heart. The most literal expression of the positioning — the file itself is the object of care, and blush earns its place inside the mark.

Strength — says "records" before it says anything else; on-strategy.

Risk — two elements inside a container; needs care to survive small sizes.

ROUTE C

Pulse

A heart carrying an ECG line. The warmest of the three and the most human, trading a little clinical formality for immediate emotional read.

Strength — warmest route; strongest fit for the pregnancy journey.

Risk — heart-plus-pulse is the most used pairing in the category.

HERO AVATAR & ICONOGRAPHY
● IN PROGRESSDIRECTION SET · ARTWORK IN PHASE 2

A doctor, not
a superhero

The single clearest instruction from the kickstart. The avatar carries the "hero" idea through competence and calm — never through costume, expression or pose.

✓ THE DIRECTION

Adult proportions. Geometric line construction at one stroke weight. No facial features. A single medical cue — the stethoscope. Calm, upright, still.

✕ NOT THIS

Cape, mask, star badge, oversized eyes, child proportions, heroic pose. Reads as a game or a cartoon and destroys medical credibility instantly.

ICON CONSTRUCTION

WHY IT MATTERS

Parents decide whether to trust a health app in about two seconds, and they decide it visually. One cartoon element anywhere in the system tells them this is a toy — and no amount of clinical copy wins that back.

POSITIONING AI
DECISION 03 · SUPPORTING TECHNOLOGY

AI is plumbing,
not the product

AI in healthcare reads as either overpromising or frightening. MyHero avoids both by never selling it — the technology is described only by the manual work it removes.

THE FOUR RULES
01

Never lead with AI

Not in the name, the app store title, the home screen or the first minute of any demo.

02

Describe it by what it removes

"Less typing." "Nothing to file." Not "smart", not "powered by", not "intelligent".

03

It never speaks for a clinician

AI summarises what a clinician has already recorded. It does not interpret, predict or advise.

04

Always show the source

Every assisted line links back to the clinical note it came from, and says who checked it.

APPROVED PHRASING

  • "Filled in from your visit — checked by your clinic."
  • "Your record updates itself after each appointment."
  • "Less paperwork for you and for the clinic."

BANNED PHRASING

  • "AI-powered health companion"
  • "Smart diagnosis" · "Predicts your health"
  • "Revolutionary" · "Next-generation" · "Cutting-edge"
THE ONE-LINE VERSION

"Your record keeps itself up to date, so you don't have to."

This is the only sentence in the brand that describes the AI, and it never uses the word.
