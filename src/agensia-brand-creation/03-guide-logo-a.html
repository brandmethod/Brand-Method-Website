Logo Guide
What is actually being made. A logo is the smallest part of it.

01   The Mark

One drawing that identifies the brand with no words attached. It has to work at 16 px, in one colour, and stitched into cloth.

Deliverable: symbol, in every approved colourway and size band.

02   The Lockup

The fixed relationship between mark and name. Once set, the spacing is not a design decision any more, it is a specification.

Deliverable: horizontal, vertical, and the clearspace rule that governs both.

03   The System

Colour, typography, layout and image. This is where the brand is actually recognised, because most surfaces never carry the mark at all.

Deliverable: palette with ratios, type scale, grid, art direction.

04   The Application

The system carried onto the surfaces the business actually uses. An identity that has not been applied has not been tested.

Deliverable: stationery, digital, environment, and the rules that hold them together.

The order matters

Each stage constrains the next. A mark chosen without knowing the applications produces an identity that has to be redrawn for the first piece of signage. The reason application sits fourth and not last is that it is the test, not the reward.

Brand Architecture
Four models. The choice is a business decision that a designer then has to draw.

Branded House

One master brand. Everything below it is a named module, never a mark of its own.

Use when the offer is one platform and every sale trades on the same reputation.

Google · FedEx · Agensia

House of Brands

Independent brands. The parent is invisible to the buyer and often deliberately so.

Use when the products serve different audiences, or one failing must not contaminate the others.

Unilever · Procter and Gamble

Endorsed

Each brand stands on its own, with the parent named beside it as a guarantee.

Use when a new offer needs its own character but has not yet earned its own trust.

Polo by Ralph Lauren · Courtyard by Marriott

Hybrid

A mix, usually the result of acquisition rather than intent.

Use when history forces it. Manage it deliberately or it becomes an accident that the market has to interpret.

Microsoft · Amazon

The cost line

Every step to the right multiplies the marketing budget, because each independent brand has to buy its own recognition. A branded house is the cheapest structure to run and the least forgiving of a bad reputation · one failure lands on everything.

Brand Family
How a single mark carries several products without becoming several brands
The three levers, in order of how much they cost you
01   Colour

Cheapest and weakest. Distinguishes at a glance, disappears in one-colour print, and fails for a colour-blind reader unless something else carries the difference too.

abab
02   Typography

A weight or a case change on the descriptor. Survives one-colour print and reproduces exactly. The workhorse of most families.

03   Form

Most expensive. A second drawing is a second mark to govern, and the family splits the moment one of them is used alone. Use only when a product genuinely serves a different buyer.

Move down the list only when the lever above it has failed a real test, not a preference. Most families that broke apart did so because form was reached for first.

The test that breaks most families

Cover the descriptor. If the four are still distinguishable, the family has drifted into four brands. If they are indistinguishable, the family is working. The descriptor is meant to be the only difference.
