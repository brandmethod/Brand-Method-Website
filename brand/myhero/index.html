Guide
Name Funnel Reference

How a name survives

A name is not chosen, it is eliminated down to. Every stage removes candidates on a single test, so the name that reaches design has already cleared meaning, memory, law and availability.

Rule of thumb

Start with 100+ candidates. Expect 50–60% to fall out at trademark and domain screening alone. Anything that reaches stage 7 must be defensible, pronounceable and free.

Working backwards

A founder-led name usually enters at stage 7 rather than stage 1. When that happens the funnel is run in reverse — the candidate is tested against every earlier stage before it is accepted, not after.

1 Establish criteriaCategory, tone, audience, must-avoid list
2 Generate 100+ candidatesDescriptive, suggestive, abstract, coined, founder
3 Meaning & tone filterCut anything that mis-signals the category
4 Pronunciation & recall testSay it, spell it, hear it back a day later
5 Trademark & domain screen50–60% typically fall out here
6 Legal & linguistic checkClass search, negative meaning in market languages
7 Shortlist 2–3 into designOnly names that can carry a mark proceed
8 Select & registerFinal name locked, filings begin

Name & Category Sample
The eight naming routes, what each one buys you, and what it costs

Descriptive

Says exactly what it does.

General Electric · Bank of America · Hotels.com+ Instant clarity  — Hard to trademark, hard to grow beyond

Suggestive

Hints at the benefit without stating it.

Netflix · Airbnb · Salesforce+ Room to expand  — Needs support to land

Abstract

No inherent meaning; meaning is built.

Kodak · Rolex · Häagen-Dazs+ Fully ownable  — Expensive to build

Coined

Invented from roots or fragments.

Verizon · Accenture · Xerox+ Clean legal path  — Zero head start

Founder

A person's name carries the brand.

Ford · Dyson · Chanel+ Authentic, personal  — Tied to one reputation

Metaphor

Borrows meaning from another world.

Amazon · Nike · Oracle+ Memorable, rich  — Can mis-signal category

Compound

Two plain words fused into one.

Facebook · PayPal · YouTube+ Clear and ownable  — Can date quickly

Possessive

Puts the user inside the name.

MyFitnessPal · MyProtein · YourStory+ Immediate ownership  — Crowded prefix
How to choose

Routes can be combined. The most durable names pair an emotional route with a descriptive lockup — the brand name carries the feeling, and a plain category line beneath it does the explaining. That split lets the name stay short without leaving the buyer guessing.

Logo GuideReference · Method
Guide
Logo Creation
Ideation → Features → Refinement
Stage 01 — Ideation

Many rough marks, no polish

Volume beats precision. Sketch every idea the brief allows — literal, abstract, letterform, symbol — without evaluating. The goal is coverage of the territory, not a finished mark.

Output: 40–80 thumbnails. Nothing is discarded at this stage.

Stage 02 — Features

Test each mark against the brief

Every surviving mark is scored on the same criteria. A mark that cannot answer all five is not refined, it is dropped — refinement never fixes a wrong concept.

DistinctiveUnlike anything in the category
ScalableHolds at 16px and on signage
RelevantSignals the right category
ReproducibleOne colour, embroidery, stamp
TimelessSurvives a decade of trends

Output: 3–5 concepts, each defensible in one sentence.

Stage 03 — Refinement

Optical correction and system build

The chosen mark is rebuilt on a construction grid, optically corrected by eye rather than by maths, then extended into the variants the brand actually needs.

Rough→→Final

Weight, corner radius and the shoulder curve change at every step. The silhouette does not.

Output: master artwork, variants, clear space, minimum sizes.

Brand Architecture
Choosing the right brand system for your business

Branded House

Monolithic

One master brand covers everything. Every product inherits the parent's trust, and every product's failure is the parent's failure.

Google — Search, Maps, Drive, Photos
FedEx — Express, Ground, Freight

Sub-Brands

Endorsed by parent

Children carry their own name but stay visibly tied to the parent. Differentiation without starting trust from zero.

Apple — iPhone, iPad, Apple TV
Sony — PlayStation, Xperia

Endorsed Brands

Free-standing

Each brand leads with its own identity and mentions the parent only as reassurance. Lets one company serve rival audiences.

Marriott — Courtyard, Residence Inn, Fairfield

House of Brands

Pluralistic

Independent brands with no visible parent. Maximum audience targeting, maximum cost — every brand is built from scratch.

P&G — Gillette, Pampers, Tide
Unilever — Dove, Ben & Jerry's

How to choose

Follow the trust, then the audience. If the parent's reputation is the reason people buy, move left. If the products serve audiences that would reject each other, move right. Most young brands belong in the second column — borrowing credibility while building their own.

Logo Development
Composite method — several symbols resolved into one form

The process starts by defining the brand's key ideas — for example protection, focus, continuity. These ideas are translated into visual symbols, treated as candidate elements.

The elements are then combined into a single design, ensuring everything works together harmoniously. Afterwards the design is refined with careful adjustments to proportions, colour and balance, making it adaptable for any application.

Finally, the logo is tested to ensure it performs well across all mediums — from digital platforms to physical materials — delivering both meaning and functionality.

Why composites work

A mark built from three deliberate ideas will always out-argue a mark drawn from one. When a client asks "why this shape?", the composite answers in three sentences instead of a shrug.

What style decides

Style is read before shape. A parent judges whether an app is serious within about two seconds, and style — not concept — is what they are judging.

Choosing a style

Rounded terminals read as warm; flat terminals read as precise. A single stroke weight throughout is what separates a designed system from a collection of icons.

Common limits

3D, drop shadows and gradients inside a symbol all fail in one-colour print, embroidery and engraving. Where a gradient is wanted, it belongs on the tile behind the symbol — never in the symbol itself.

Detailing & Direction
How much of the idea to leave in — three levels of abstraction
Level 01 — Semi-abstract

Most detail

Every idea survives. Richest story, but the first to break at 24px and the hardest to embroider or stamp.

Level 02 — Simple  ·  recommended

Balanced

Two ideas, one shape. Reads instantly, survives every size and reproduction method, still has something to explain.

Level 03 — Most abstract

Least detail

Cleanest and most scalable, but the medical cue is gone — it could belong to an insurer, a bank or a VPN.

Where most brands land

Level 02. Level 01 loses the app icon and the embroidery; Level 03 loses the category and could belong to anyone. The rule that falls out of this: one container plus one cue — never two cues, never none.

Concept Direction
One base symbol, taken in different directions by what you combine it with

This process explores how a single base symbol can be taken in several directions while retaining one core identity and positioning. By integrating distinct, theme-like combinations for a corporate look or tenant, for a community-focused approach the character evolves to convey unique meanings.

Each direction aligns with the brand's values, ensuring consistency across all variations — and gives the client a real choice between routes rather than between drawings of the same route.

The discipline

The base symbol never changes shape between directions. Only the second element changes. If the base has to be redrawn to fit, the direction is fighting the concept.

Stylescape GuideReference · Method
Guide
Stylescape Method
Three tiers of the same idea, so the client chooses a temperature — not a picture

A moodboard asks "do you like this?". A stylescape asks a better question: how far do you want to go?

The same strategy is expressed at three intensities. The client is not choosing images, they are choosing a position on a dial — and that position becomes a brief the design team can build against.

Each tier fixes

Colour temperature · contrast level · type weight · image treatment · white space ratio · the emotional register

Why three

Two options is a coin toss. Four is paralysis. Three gives a clear middle and two honest edges — and most clients pick the middle once they have seen both extremes.

EXTREME

High contrast, high confidence

Deep ground, bold weight, minimal colour. Reads as authority. Risk: can feel cold or corporate for a consumer product.

MID  ·  RECOMMENDED

Clear, warm, unhurried

Light ground, one colour carrying the system, generous white space, a single warm accent. The tier most brands end up choosing.

LIGHT

Soft, tactile, domestic

Accent-led, low contrast, rounded everything. Warmest and most personal. Risk: gives away authority the category may require.

Reading the tree

Depth is deliberate. Anything at level three is a module, not a brand — it never gets its own mark, its own colour or its own name in the app store. Only MyHero is marketed.

Content & Design Reference
The register to write in, and the register to design in
Content reference — how it should read

"Your next scan is on Wednesday 3 September, 10:30am."

Weekday and month spelled out. No codes, no room numbers up front.

"Everything in this test looked normal."

The answer first. Raw values are available underneath, never above.

"Dr Amira would like to go through one of these with you at your next visit."

Names the person. Never alarms a parent a clinician has not yet spoken to.

"Nothing here yet. Your clinic will add to this after your next visit."

Empty states explain what happens next, rather than reporting zero results.

"AI-generated clinical summary · 0 results returned · ⚠ ABNORMAL VALUE FLAGGED"

All three break a rule: names the technology, reports like a database, alarms without a clinician.
