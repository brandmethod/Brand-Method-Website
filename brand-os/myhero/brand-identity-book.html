Introduction & Terms

Purpose of this guide. This is the MyHero Brand Identity Book. It defines who we are, what we stand for, and how the brand appears wherever a patient meets it. It exists so every screen, slip, post, and reply reads as one brand, whoever makes it.

How to use it. The book is a system, not a suggestion. Follow the rules as written. When a situation is not covered, borrow the closest rule and confirm with the brand desk before it ships. In a health product this matters more than usual: an inconsistency here does not just look careless, it costs trust at the exact moment trust is being decided.

Who it is for. Elite Care Clinic staff, the development team, appointed agencies, and any authorised partner producing anything that carries the MyHero name.

01 · Ownership

All marks, assets, and creatives in this book are the intellectual property of the MyHero owning entity.

02 · Usage rights

Licensed for MyHero business use by internal teams and authorised partners only.

03 · Confidentiality

This book and its assets are confidential. No external sharing without written approval.

04 · Acceptance

Using any asset means accepting these terms. Full detail sits in Governance · 6.1.

01 · Our Brand · 1.1

Who We Are

MyHero is a personal medical record app for families managing ongoing care. It takes the history that currently lives in a clinic filing cabinet — visits, prescriptions, results, allergies — and puts it in the hand of the person it belongs to. It is built by a practising doctor, which is why it holds what a clinic actually needs, and read by parents at eleven at night, which is why it says it plainly.

01

Records first

The product is a medical record people can actually hold. Everything else, the AI included, exists in service of that. We are not an AI company that happens to store health data.

02

Credible, not cold

Medical seriousness without the grey weight of a government form. Accuracy is the floor; warmth is what makes people open it twice.

03

Built for whoever carries the file

Designed for the parent in the waiting room, not the specialist at the desk. If a tired non-medical adult cannot follow it, it is not finished.

04

Technology behind the doctor

AI supports the record. It never fronts it, never diagnoses, and never speaks with more confidence than the evidence allows.

01 · Our Brand · 1.2

Our Story

The file should follow the patient, not the clinic.

MyHero started with something a practising doctor sees at every single follow-up: the consultation that begins from zero. The same history re-explained. The same prescriptions re-listed from memory or from a photo on a cracked phone screen. A file that exists — accurate, complete, careful — but lives in a cabinet the patient will never open.

The frustration

Every follow-up starts over. Patients carry folders, photograph prescriptions, and still arrive without the one detail that matters. The clinic re-enters what it already knows.

The idea

One secure record the patient owns and carries. Visits, prescriptions, results and allergies in one place, on the phone already in their hand, in language they can read without a translator.

The shift

From record-keeping done to patients, to a record patients hold. Less manual entry for the clinic, less repetition for the family, and a history that arrives before the consultation does.

02 · Strategy · 2.2

Positioning & Messaging

Message hierarchy — in this order, always
01 · What

One secure place for your medical records.

02 · Who

For families managing ongoing care.

03 · Why us

Built by a practising doctor, so it holds what a clinic actually needs.

The AI messaging rule

AI is named as supporting technology, never as the headline and never in the first line of any asset. No diagnostic claims. No prediction language. "Smart" is never a substitute for "accurate". If a sentence would still be true with the AI removed, lead with that sentence.

Words

We use: record, history, prescription, dosage, reminder, secure, yours, your doctor.

We avoid: superhero, powered by AI, revolutionary, smart diagnosis, patient portal, e-government, seamless, disrupt.

03 · Visual Identity · 3.1

Logo Overview

Primary lockup
The standard

The mark carries a medical product, so it is judged against a standard rather than a preference. It must read as medical before any word is read, hold warmth without becoming playful, and survive the places a clinic actually prints it.

The mark must

  • Read as medical at 24px, before any word is read
  • Carry warmth without becoming playful
  • Survive one colour on a carbon-copy prescription slip
  • Work as a square app icon without being redrawn
  • Sit beside the Elite Care Clinic mark without competing

The mark must never

  • Read as a game icon or an app-store cartoon
  • Use a cape, crest, shield or superhero device
  • Read as a ministry seal or government emblem
  • Rely on a generic cross, stethoscope or heartbeat line
  • Depend on a gradient to be recognisable

03 · Visual Identity · 3.3

Clearspace, Sizing & Don'ts

Clearspace

The clear area on all four sides equals the cap height of the wordmark's M. Nothing enters it — no type, no rule, no photograph edge, no other logo.

Minimum sizes
Print
12 mm wide for the full lockup
Screen
96 px wide for the full lockup
Mark alone
24 px — below this, use the app icon
Icon master
1024 × 1024 px, square, no transparency
Never

01 Stretch, condense or rotate it.

02 Recolour it outside the four approved versions.

03 Add a drop shadow, glow, bevel or outline.

04 Place it on a busy photograph without a holding shape.

05 Bolt on a cross, stethoscope, heartbeat or cape.

06 Set the wordmark in a substitute typeface.

07 Lock it to another logo without approval.

08 Use it as a bullet, pattern or texture.

03 · Visual Identity · 3.5

Typography

Three roles, one job each, so hierarchy stays obvious on a small screen held at arm's length.

Headline

Aa Bb Cc

A humanist grotesque. Confident, never editorial. Screen titles and section heads.

Body & interface

Aa Bb Cc

Highly legible at 15–17px. Open apertures, tall x-height, unambiguous 1 / l / I.

Data & dosage

Aa 0123

Monospace with tabular figures. Record numbers, dosages, dates, times.

Hierarchy
Display
32 / 36 · bold · −3.5% tracking
Title
22 / 28 · semibold · −2% tracking
Body
16 / 24 · regular
Caption
13 / 18 · regular
Label
11 / 14 · mono · +13% tracking · caps
The dosage legibility floor

Non-negotiable, and it overrides any design preference: dosage, dose-time, allergy and record-number text is never set below 16px, never lighter than regular weight, and never tracked tighter than zero. If a layout cannot hold that, the layout changes.

03 · Visual Identity · 3.6

The Hero Icon & Avatar

The distance between a trustworthy clinician and a cartoon superhero is not a matter of taste — it is the difference between a medical product and a game. The line is drawn here.

Trustworthy clinician

  • Adult proportions — roughly 1:7 head to body
  • Calm, neutral expression. Approachable, not performing
  • Clinical dress: coat or scrubs, rendered simply
  • Standing or seated at rest, front-facing
  • One accent colour over a neutral base
  • Readable as a silhouette at 24px

Cartoon superhero

  • Cape, mask, chest emblem or badge
  • Oversized head, big eyes, chibi proportions
  • Action pose, flying, fists on hips
  • Sparkles, motion lines, impact bursts
  • More than two accent colours
  • Anything that would sit in a game's store listing
Avatar set

Three variations for in-app use: default, greeting, and empty-state. Same figure, same proportions, different posture only.

Small sizes

Below 32px the figure is replaced by the app icon. The doctor is never simplified into a cartoon to survive shrinking.

Never

The hero icon is never the app icon, never the logo, and never appears next to a medical result or dosage.

UI Foundations

RoleColourRule
SurfaceWhiteThe record always sits on white. Never on a tinted panel.
CardBlue 4%Groups related entries. Maximum two nesting levels.
Primary actionSky BlueOne per screen. If there are two, one is not primary.
AccentRoseHighlights and empty states only. Never a button.

Warmth rules

Corner radius 8px — rounded enough to be human, not so round it reads as a children's app. Generous whitespace. Sentence case everywhere; block capitals never used as body text.

Microcopy

Yes: "Added by Elite Care Clinic, 12 Aug."
No: "Record ingested successfully."

Accessibility floor

Contrast 4.5:1 on all text. Tap targets 44px. Supports 200% text scaling — many users are managing a parent's records, not their own.

Spacing & motion

4px base unit, 8px rhythm. Motion under 200ms, ease-out, opacity and position only. Nothing bounces. Nothing celebrates.

Always marked. Every AI-generated element carries the AI-Assisted colour and the label. No silent AI.

05 · Applications · 5.2

Digital & Store Presence

Enough to launch credibly. Health content carries a duty of care that most brands do not, so the rules are tighter than usual.

Profile set
Avatar
App icon, unchanged, on all platforms
Cover
One line of positioning on a solid ground. No collage.
Bio
What it is, who it is for, and that a doctor built it. In that order.
Cadence
2–3 posts a week. Consistency over volume.
Three post templates

01 · How-to. One feature, one screen, one sentence.

02 · Plain answer. A common question about records or prescriptions, answered without selling.

03 · From the clinic. Dr Amira on something she sees in practice. Never a testimonial about the app.

Content duty of care

No medical advice, ever — only information about the product and general record-keeping. No patient stories, no before-and-afters, no screenshots containing real data. Nothing that implies the app improves a health outcome.

Launch frame

One announcement asset in the four required ratios: 4:5 feed, 1:1 feed, 9:16 story, 1200×630 link. Same copy across all four.

06 · Governance · 6.1

Legal, Usage & Approvals

Using the identity means accepting the system: the rules in this book are the licence terms. Anything outside them needs written approval before it ships.

01 · The mark

MyHero is always set as the logo or in caps. Never conjugated, pluralised, or blended into a sentence.

02 · Lockups

Partner and co-brand lockups require brand-desk approval: equal optical height, 2× clearspace.

03 · Licensing

Assets are licensed for MyHero business use. No resale, no template redistribution, no AI training.

04 · Translation

Malay and English may change the words, never the system: same grid, same colour, same voice.

05 · Health claims & patient data

No asset may claim a health outcome, a diagnosis, or clinical accuracy beyond "records as entered by your clinic". No real patient data appears in any mockup, screenshot, or presentation, in any state of anonymisation. Every public-facing claim about security or compliance is reviewed by the clinic's legal counsel before it ships — not by the brand desk.

01 · Request

Submit the artwork

Send the file, the channel, and the run date to the brand custodian.

02 · Review

System check

Checked against this book: logo, colour, type, layout, voice — and the claims rule above.

03 · Approve

Sign-off

Written approval within three working days. No sign-off, no ship.
