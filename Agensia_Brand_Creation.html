Why a funnel and not a brainstorm

A brainstorm produces names the room likes. A funnel produces names that survive contact with a register, a search engine and a competitor. The gates are ordered so the cheapest disqualification happens first.

Where most projects fail

At gate 5. A name that has cleared every creative test and no legal test has cost the project a month. Legal screening moves earlier the more crowded the category is.

Gate 7 is a decision, not a discovery

The funnel does not choose. It removes everything that cannot be chosen, and hands the client a set where every option is defensible. The choice that remains is a strategic one.

Agensia entered this document at gate 7. The name is confirmed and registered in Brand Book V3.0. The pages that follow record why it holds, not whether it should.

Name & Category Sample
Six name types, what each one buys you and what each one costs

Descriptive

Says what the company does, in the words a buyer would use.

WorkflowEngine · ComplyAI · AutoFlowBuys instant comprehension. Costs ownership: hard to register, impossible to defend, and dates the moment the product widens.

Suggestive

Points at the benefit without naming the mechanism.

Salesforce · Slack · StripeThe best commercial balance. Understood on second contact, still ownable. Most enterprise brands should sit here.

Coined

Built from a root and a suffix. Means nothing before you fill it.

Agensia · Celonis · VerizonHighest ownership, widest legal room. Costs launch effort: the meaning has to be supplied by the brand, not the word.

Compound

Two real words fused into one new one.

Salesforce · Facebook · PayPalReads instantly and registers well. Risk of sounding assembled rather than designed if the halves fight each other.

Founder or heritage

A person, a place or a date.

Siemens · Rolls Royce · BoschCarries credibility and continuity. Costs flexibility: the story is fixed, and a founder exit becomes a brand problem.

Acronym

Initials standing in for a longer descriptive name.

IBM · SAP · ABBOnly works after decades of spend, or as a shortening the market invents. Never a starting position.
Reading this page

Ownership rises left to right. Comprehension falls in the same direction. The choice is always how much launch effort you are willing to spend to own the word outright · and in a regulated category, where the buyer reads before they feel, a coined name has to be paired with a descriptive line that carries the category.

Seven tests. A name that fails two of them will be replaced within five years.

The two that get skipped

Durable and Drawable. Both are invisible at the moment of choosing and both are expensive later. A name that dates to a technology cycle has to be replaced; a name that will not draw forces the identity to carry weight the word should have carried.

Two failures is the threshold

One failure is survivable and usually manageable with a descriptive lockup. Two compound: a name that is neither ownable nor distinct has no defence and no recall, and no amount of design will supply either.

Seven of seven, with one standing action. See Why The Name in the client section for the worked assessment, including the register position that is held with legal rather than closed.

Logo Guide
What is actually being made. A logo is the smallest part of it.

01   The Mark

One drawing that identifies the brand with no words attached. It has to work at 16 px, in one colour, and stitched into cloth.

Deliverable: symbol, in every approved colourway and size band.

02   The Lockup

The fixed relationship between mark and name. Once set, the spacing is not a design decision any more, it is a specification.

Deliverable: horizontal, vertical, and the clearspace rule that governs both.

03   The System

Colour, typography, layout and image. This is where the brand is actually recognised, because most surfaces never carry the mark at all.

Deliverable: palette with ratios, type scale, grid, art direction.

04   The Application

The system carried onto the surfaces the business actually uses. An identity that has not been applied has not been tested.

Deliverable: stationery, digital, environment, and the rules that hold them together.

The order matters

Each stage constrains the next. A mark chosen without knowing the applications produces an identity that has to be redrawn for the first piece of signage. The reason application sits fourth and not last is that it is the test, not the reward.

Brand Architecture
Four models. The choice is a business decision that a designer then has to draw.

Branded House

One master brand. Everything below it is a named module, never a mark of its own.

Use when the offer is one platform and every sale trades on the same reputation.

Google · FedEx · Agensia

House of Brands

Independent brands. The parent is invisible to the buyer and often deliberately so.

Use when the products serve different audiences, or one failing must not contaminate the others.

Unilever · Procter and Gamble

Endorsed

Each brand stands on its own, with the parent named beside it as a guarantee.

Use when a new offer needs its own character but has not yet earned its own trust.

Polo by Ralph Lauren · Courtyard by Marriott

Hybrid

A mix, usually the result of acquisition rather than intent.

Use when history forces it. Manage it deliberately or it becomes an accident that the market has to interpret.

Microsoft · Amazon

The cost line

Every step to the right multiplies the marketing budget, because each independent brand has to buy its own recognition. A branded house is the cheapest structure to run and the least forgiving of a bad reputation · one failure lands on everything.

Brand Family
How a single mark carries several products without becoming several brands
The three levers, in order of how much they cost you
01   Colour

Cheapest and weakest. Distinguishes at a glance, disappears in one-colour print, and fails for a colour-blind reader unless something else carries the difference too.

A weight or a case change on the descriptor. Survives one-colour print and reproduces exactly. The workhorse of most families.

Most expensive. A second drawing is a second mark to govern, and the family splits the moment one of them is used alone. Use only when a product genuinely serves a different buyer.

Logo Features & Meaning
Every formal decision inside a mark is read by the viewer, whether or not it was intended
Curve

Reads as human, adaptive, approachable. Softens any amount of technical content set beside it.

Agensia: the whole mark
Angle

Reads as engineered, decided, precise. Also reads as hard, and at volume as cold.

Agensia: the type, not the mark
Symmetry

Reads as stable, balanced, trustworthy. Threefold symmetry reads as systemic rather than merely tidy.

Agensia: threefold
Repetition

Reads as process, iteration, convergence. The eye follows the passes and infers a method behind them.

Agensia: the contour set
Counter space

The hole in the middle. Reads as openness and access, and mechanically it is what keeps a dense mark legible small.

Agensia: the aperture
Line weight

Thin reads as precise and premium. Heavy reads as durable and loud. Thin marks die first at small sizes.

Agensia: thin, so reduction is specified
Enclosure

Reads as protection and membership. Also reads as institution, and it costs you the app icon.

Agensia: none, deliberately
Direction

Upward or rightward reads as progress. Downward or leftward reads as decline, regardless of intent.

Agensia: upward resolve
The rule this page exists to enforce

Every feature above is a claim. A mark should be able to justify each one, and any feature that cannot be justified is decoration the client will eventually ask you to remove.

Where meaning goes wrong

Meaning stacks. Two features can each be right and together say something neither intended. A curve plus an enclosure plus an upward angle is a shield with a chevron in it, whatever the brief said.

Logo Development
Six stages. Each one narrows, and none of them is skipped.
01   Elements

Break the strategy into drawable parts. Containers, cues, letterforms. Nothing is composed yet.

Output: element table
02   Combination

Pair the elements. Name each pairing, and state what it would mean before drawing it.

Output: combo table
03   Sketch

Draw four variants per surviving combination. Quantity here is what buys quality later.

Output: sketch pages
04   Construction

Rebuild the chosen sketch on a grid. Ratio, stroke, curve rule, optical correction.

Output: construction sheet
05   Refinement

Test at every size and in one colour. Fix what breaks. Most of the work happens here and none of it shows.

Output: the final mark
agensia
06   System

Lockups, clearspace, colourways, minimum sizes, don'ts. The mark becomes a specification.

Output: the brand book
Where clients want to start

At stage 3, because that is the first stage with pictures in it. A mark that starts at stage 3 has no element table behind it, which means there is no answer to "why this shape" other than taste. Stages 1 and 2 exist to make the eventual choice defensible in a room the designer is not in.

Method
Logo Exploration
One idea, varied on purpose rather than at random. Weight, colour, density, rotation, centre.
What is being varied

Contour count, stroke weight, spacing between passes, rotation, colour, and whether the centre is filled or open. One variable moves at a time, so a comparison is possible.

Why a grid and not a page of favourites

A page of favourites tells you what the designer liked. A grid tells you where the idea stops working, which is the more useful information.

Read the grid squinting

Step back until the detail disappears. What survives is the silhouette, and the silhouette is what a viewer actually recognises at distance or at 20 px.

The Agensia exploration converged on high contour count, thin stroke, open centre. Every other combination either goes muddy at small size or loses the aperture.

Detailing & Direction
The decisions that happen after the shape is right and before the mark is finished
Optical correction, four cases
Overshoot

A circle drawn to the same height as a square looks smaller. Round forms are drawn slightly larger so they read as equal.

Optical centre

The mathematical centre sits low. Elements are lifted by roughly two per cent of the height to look centred.

Stroke compensation

Horizontals read heavier than verticals at the same width, so they are drawn thinner.

Joint relief

Where two strokes meet, ink pools and the joint goes black at small sizes. The corner is cut back before it does.

Direction, the four questions that settle a mark
01   Where does the eye land first?

If it lands on a detail rather than the silhouette, the detail is too loud. In a contour mark, the aperture must win.

02   What is the second reading?

A good mark gives something back on the second look. In Agensia's case the A resolves out of the contours once the viewer stops seeing a pattern.

03   What breaks first?

Every mark has a failure point. Knowing it lets you specify around it. Here it is contour count, which is why the count is a size-band rule rather than a scale.

04   Can someone else redraw it?

If the construction cannot be described in a sentence, the mark will drift the first time it is rebuilt by someone outside the studio.

Why this page is not optional

None of it is visible in a presentation and all of it is visible on a wall, on a badge and at 16 px. Detailing is the difference between a shape and a mark.

01   Curve set

The single generating curve is fixed: start point, end point, tension. Every later pass is a transform of this one line, which is what makes the mark reproducible rather than traced.

02   Offset spacing

The gap between passes decides whether the mark reads as line or as mass. Too tight and it fills in at small size; too loose and the shape stops resolving into an A.

03   Rotation

Three copies at 120 degrees. The rotation point is not the centre of the artwork but the optical centre of the lobe cluster, which sits slightly high.

04   Density

Contour count is tuned per size band, not scaled. This is the single decision that keeps the mark alive between 1024 px and 16 px.

05   The mark

Aperture held open, joints relieved, overshoot corrected. Confirmed as the Agensia symbol in Brand Book V3.0 and reproduced from that artwork throughout this document.

What refinement is not

It is not another round of options. By this stage the idea is settled and the work is executional. A client who is still choosing at refinement has been given options too late, and the schedule is already lost.

The contrast rule most brands break

A signature colour is chosen for its presence, not its legibility, and then somebody sets a headline in it. Green on white measures 1.7 to 1, which is a fail at any text size.

Never for text
Green on paper  1.7:1 Green on mist  1.6:1 Paper on green  1.7:1

Where green text is genuinely unavoidable, the system substitutes Green Deep #6FA800 and the ratio is checked before use. Everything green in this document that carries a word uses Green Deep for exactly that reason.

The other half of the rule

Colour alone must never carry meaning. Every status in the system pairs a colour with a shape or a word, so that the message survives greyscale print and a colour-blind reader.

What a brandscape is for

Not to find a gap for its own sake. To make sure a difference the strategy already claims is visible at a glance, and to stop the studio drawing something the category has drawn nine times.

The axes are chosen, not given

Pick the two dimensions the buyer actually discriminates on. In enterprise automation those are how literal the mark is about machinery, and how much warmth it allows itself.

An empty quadrant is not always an opportunity

Sometimes it is empty because it does not work. Test the gap against the strategy before treating it as a finding.

Agensia sits abstract and technical, with warmth held in the curve rather than in the palette. The upper right is genuinely thin, and the reason is that most platforms in this category default to literal machinery.

Naming a module

Plain nouns that describe the job. Studio, Assure, Console, Fabric. A module name is not a place to be inventive, because every invented module name is a name the market has to learn on top of the one it is already learning.

Method
Stylescape Guide
Three tiers of the same strategy. The client picks a temperature, not a picture.
EXTREME

The furthest the strategy can be pushed and still be true

Maximum contrast, maximum conviction. It exists to show the client where the edge is, so that the middle reads as a choice rather than as caution. Roughly one project in six actually selects it.

MID

The strategy delivered at working temperature

Where most brands should land, and where most of them do. Not a compromise between the other two: it is the position, drawn without either exaggeration. The tier that has to survive ten years of daily use.

LIGHT

The softest reading the strategy will bear

Included so the client can see what is given away by going gentle. It is not a safe option, it is the option that trades authority for approachability, and in a regulated category that trade is usually the wrong one.

Why three and not five

Three is the smallest set that establishes a range. Five turns a decision into a survey, and the client starts combining rows, which is how a stylescape becomes a mood board.

The columns are fixed

Every tier is shown across the same five columns: colour and texture, hero and art direction, font and branding, app and icon, environment. Comparison is only possible if nothing else varies.

The instruction to the client

Do not pick images. Pick a temperature. Nothing in a stylescape is a design · it is a specification of feeling, which the design then has to meet.

Colour territory

Blue dominant  ·  orange and purple as the exceptions  ·  black for platform brands  ·  green open

Question 4 matters most. In a category this crowded with blue geometry, confusion is the default outcome rather than the risk.

One curve, many passes. Elements 1 to 4 are not four ingredients to be mixed, they are four properties of a single construction. Nothing is added to the mark: depth is produced by repetition, never by a gradient, a shadow or a second element.

So the client can see what was considered and why it was set aside. An element table is the answer to "why this shape" in a room the designer is not in, and rows 6 to 10 are half of that answer.
