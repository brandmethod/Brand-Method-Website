Method
Stylescape Guide
Three tiers of the same strategy. The client picks a temperature, not a picture.
EXTREME

The furthest the strategy can be pushed and still be true

Maximum contrast, maximum conviction. It exists to show the client where the edge is, so that the middle reads as a choice rather than as caution. Roughly one project in six actually selects it.

MID

The strategy delivered at working temperature

Where most brands should land, and where most of them do. Not a compromise between the other two: it is the position, drawn without either exaggeration. The tier that has to survive ten years of daily use.

LIGHT

The softest reading the strategy will bear

Included so the client can see what is given away by going gentle. It is not a safe option, it is the option that trades authority for approachability, and in a regulated category that trade is usually the wrong one.

Why three and not five

Three is the smallest set that establishes a range. Five turns a decision into a survey, and the client starts combining rows, which is how a stylescape becomes a mood board.

The columns are fixed

Every tier is shown across the same five columns: colour and texture, hero and art direction, font and branding, app and icon, environment. Comparison is only possible if nothing else varies.

The instruction to the client

Do not pick images. Pick a temperature. Nothing in a stylescape is a design · it is a specification of feeling, which the design then has to meet.

What goes in each of the five columns, and what each column is actually testing
01   Colour + Texture

The palette in its working proportion, plus whatever surface the brand lives on.

Tests whether the ratio holds. A palette looks fine as five squares and wrong the moment one of them is 46 per cent of the page.

HERO IMAGE
02   Hero + Art Direction

The image the brand leads with, and one line of headline set on it.

Tests whether the tone survives a photograph. This is the column clients react to hardest and the one that decides the photography budget.

agensia
03   Font + Branding

The display face at size, and the lockup as it will actually be used.

Tests whether type and mark belong to each other. Two good choices can still be two choices rather than one system.

04   App + Icon

The icon at real size, and one interface surface.

Tests the smallest and most repeated appearance of the brand. A direction that cannot make an icon has a problem no amount of print will hide.

ENVIRONMENT
05   Environment

The physical space the brand appears in, or the space its customers work in.

Tests whether the direction is honest about where the product actually lives. A factory floor is not a co-working space, and a palette can tell the difference.

How to run the review

Present all three tiers at once and say nothing for thirty seconds. The tier the client looks at longest is rarely the one they name, and the difference between those two is the most useful information in the meeting.

What a stylescape is not

Not a homepage, not a proposal, and not a promise about any specific image. Placeholders stay visibly placeholders for exactly that reason · an approved stylescape must never be mistaken for approved artwork.
