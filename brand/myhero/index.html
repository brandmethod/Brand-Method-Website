Brand ambition readyReads at the scale of a national consumer health brand, not a single clinic's internal tool.

The name positions the brand not as a product, but as a role the parent already plays.

In one sentence, it makes room for

— A record that is theirs, not the clinic's
— A tone that reassures instead of instructing
— A future beyond one clinic

What the name deliberately avoids

Any reference to artificial intelligence, any clinical Latin, any word that would date the product to a technology cycle, and any construction that sounds like a government service.

Decision status

Aligned at kickstart — the team reached consensus on the descriptive name My Health Record, with MyHero as the spoken and searched brand.

Reading the tree

Depth is deliberate. Anything at level three is a module, not a brand — it never gets its own mark, its own colour or its own name in the app store. Only MyHero is marketed.

The Record Engine never surfaces. It has no logo, no name in the interface and no mention in marketing. When a parent sees its output, the credit goes to the clinic — "checked by your clinic" — never to the system.

If MyHero later serves clinics beyond Elite Care, the parent node changes and everything below it survives intact. This is the main reason the name avoids any clinic-specific reference.

Colour territory

Blue is the category default — it signals clinical safety, which is exactly why almost everyone reaches for it. MyHero cannot differentiate on hue. Sky stays, because the client chose it and it is correct; the separation has to come from form, warmth and typography.

Blue dominant · teal and blush secondary · black for platform brands

Form language

The category splits two ways: government and telehealth marks are geometric and cool; femtech and paediatric marks are rounded and soft, often to the point of reading as a toy.

The kickstart put MyHero between them — credible enough for a clinic, warm enough for a parent. That gap is the opportunity, and it is genuinely under-occupied.

What to read for

When the artwork lands, judge each mark on four things only:

1  Does it survive at 24px?
2  Does it read medical without reading governmental?
3  Would a tired parent trust it?
4  Could it be mistaken for one of the others?

Question 4 is the one that matters most — a crowded blue category makes confusion the default outcome.

The rule that governs both rows

One drawing per size band, not one drawing scaled. Below 32px the cue is simplified and the stroke thickens; above 64px it thins again. A single vector used everywhere will look wrong at both ends.

Blush is not an alert

The blush treatment exists for milestones and the pregnancy journey only. It must never carry a warning, a result or a system state — that is what amber and red are for.
